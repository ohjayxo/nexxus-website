What your primes check

Before a prime awards you a subcontract that involves controlled unclassified information (CUI), DFARS 252.204-7020 says it has to confirm you’ve completed a Basic Assessment within the last three years. That assessment is the score you post in SPRS, the government’s Supplier Performance Risk System.

A Basic Assessment is defined as your own review of your System Security Plan. When you post the score, you also post the date you expect to reach the full 110. Both are representations you’re making to the government.

Is your posted score based on a real System Security Plan, or an estimate?

If the honest answer is “an estimate,” or you’re not sure, that’s where we start.

Worth knowing before your next prime questionnaire

Most of what goes wrong isn’t the firewall. It’s the paperwork, the scope, and the accuracy of what’s been said.

Enforcement didn’t pause.
Federal settlements with defense contractors and a university have turned on inaccurate cybersecurity statements, not on breaches. Getting hacked wasn’t the trigger. Saying something inaccurate was.
No plan, no assessment.
Under the DoD Assessment Methodology, a missing System Security Plan means the assessment couldn’t be completed. The plan isn’t background paperwork; it’s what the assessment is built on.
A plan of action earns no points.
A requirement that isn’t implemented is scored as not implemented, whether or not you have a plan to fix it.
Older machines aren’t disqualifying.
Equipment that can’t meet a requirement, like an older machine controller, can be documented as an enduring exception: isolated, and described in the System Security Plan with its mitigations. Age alone isn’t a barrier.
Level 1 has no grace period.
At Level 1, a plan of action isn’t allowed at any point. All 15 requirements in FAR 52.204-21 need to be in place before the annual affirmation is signed.

Common questions

Is CMMC cancelled?

No. Third-party certification is paused. Self-assessments, your SPRS score, and the DFARS clauses behind them are still in force.

Will you certify us, or tell us we’ll pass?

No. We’re not a certifying assessor, and we don’t predict outcomes. We write the documentation; you review it and post your own score.

Do you need to see our drawings or contracts?

No. We map where controlled information lives without taking custody of any of it. Please don’t send drawings, contracts, or controlled information by email or any other way.

We only handle Federal Contract Information, not CUI. Do we need you?

Probably not. Our engagements are built around Level 2 documentation. One thing worth knowing either way: at Level 1, a plan of action isn’t allowed, so all 15 requirements need to be in place before the affirmation is signed.

Do you replace our IT provider?

No. We document, and your IT provider implements. The plan of action in the full write-up gives them a clear list to work from.

What does it cost?
